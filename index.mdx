Sprint Timing is a native Android app for measuring a sprint across three timing lines. Each camera phone detects one crossing locally, timestamps it from the camera frame, and exchanges only timing and synchronization data over Wi-Fi.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Three timing points" icon="route">
    Place one phone at START, one at SPLIT, and one at FINISH. The app sorts the three timestamps and calculates split and total time.
  </Card>

  <Card title="No video streaming" icon="video-off">
    Camera analysis stays on each phone. Only timestamps, device status, controls, and final results are sent over the network.
  </Card>

  <Card title="GNSS-first clock model" icon="satellite">
    Each phone maps its monotonic clock to GNSS time. When all three mappings qualify, results use the shared GNSS time domain.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Wi-Fi fallback" icon="wifi">
    Repeated two-way UDP exchanges estimate peer clock offset and uncertainty when GNSS is unavailable on one or more phones.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Device roles

| Device | Default role | Camera default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google Pixel 7 | START | Rear |
| Huawei EML-L29 | SPLIT | Device default |
| OnePlus Nord 2T | FINISH | Front |
| Other Android device | DISPLAY | Camera and GNSS disabled |

The role button on a camera phone can cycle between START, SPLIT, and FINISH. Other Android devices automatically open the receive-only display interface, which can arm all timing phones, change detector sensitivity, toggle previews, and store recent results.

## How a run is measured

<Steps>
  <Step title="Detect locally">
    Camera2 provides YUV frames and source-frame timestamps. A narrow, three-zone luma detector confirms directional movement across the virtual timing line.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Map the clocks">
    Each event is mapped to GNSS time when the local GNSS model is locked or degraded. Peer-to-peer Wi-Fi clock estimates remain available as a fallback.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect three marks">
    The FINISH phone receives one timestamp from each role. Packet arrival order is ignored.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Calculate the result">
    The three values are sorted. The second minus the first is the split; the third minus the first is the total time. The app also reports uncertainty and confidence.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  This is a device-focused MVP. Camera timestamp quality, delivered frame rate, GNSS behavior, Wi-Fi timing, thermal behavior, and end-to-end accuracy must be validated on the physical phones.
</Note>
